Mobile calendar — 10 ways to show it

The desktop month grid works, but on a ~375 px phone a 7-column grid with photos inside the cells collapses into unreadable thumbnails, and two different events on back-to-back days (Aug 7–8 vs Aug 9) blur together. Below is a short study of how the best apps solve this, followed by ten mobile-only prototypes on the real JBY events. Nothing here touches the live site.

The problem, precisely

A month has 7 columns. On a phone each cell is roughly 40–48 px wide. That is fine for a number and a dot — but far too small for a photo, a title, or to distinguish a two-day event from a one-day event beside it. Putting imagery in the cell (our current desktop approach) is the root of the “tiny picture” issue.

What the best apps actually do

Google Calendar / Apple Calendar: on phones they default to a Schedule / Agenda or Day view. The month grid, when shown, uses tiny coloured dots — never content — and multi-day events render as continuous coloured bars. You tap a day to reveal its events below.

Luma (lu.ma) & Eventbrite: mobile is a vertical agenda — rich cards (image, title, date, place) grouped by date. No grid at all.

Airbnb / Resy / OpenTable: a horizontal date rail (scrollable week) with the selected day’s list underneath.

Notion Calendar / Fantastical: a compact month plus a detail pane; multi-day events as spanning pills.

Principles for JBY mobile

  1. No photos inside month cells. Reserve imagery for the list / detail, where it has room to be beautiful.
  2. Signal events with colour + dots or bars. Colour is what lets neighbouring events read as separate.
  3. Make multi-day continuous. A spanning bar (not a lone dot) says “this is one event across these days,” and a different colour next door says “this is a different event.”
  4. Default to a list; treat the grid as an overview. Browsing happens in the agenda; the grid is for orientation.
  5. Progressive disclosure. Overview → tap a day → detail.
  6. Thumb-friendly. Tap targets ≥ 44 px; keep key actions low on screen.
  7. Keep context sticky (month / selected day) while scrolling.
Watch the Aug 7–8 (Lake Tahoe Concours) vs Aug 9 (Riva Soirée) case in each prototype — that consecutive, two-different-events situation is the real test.
