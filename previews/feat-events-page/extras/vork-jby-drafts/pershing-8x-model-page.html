Overview

The page starts with what buyers need to decide.

A model detail page should not make buyers hunt for the basics. This structure brings the most important signals to the top, then expands into imagery, lifestyle, layout, technical data, equipment, and enquiry.

01

Immediate identity

Brand, model, yacht type, and positioning are visible in the first screen so visitors understand exactly where they are.

02

Decision specs

Length, speed, engines, cabins, material, and capacity sit beside the hero image because they drive comparison and fit.

03

Clear next step

Pricing, consultation, gallery, and specifications are never buried. The page is designed to move naturally from research to enquiry.

Gallery

Exterior first, then details.

For a yacht model, imagery is not decoration. It is evidence. The gallery is grouped so visitors can evaluate stance, deck flow, stern platform, and onboard lifestyle without scrolling through an unlabelled image dump.

Highlights

Feature sections explain benefits, not just parts.

Instead of a loose list of features, each section gives one buyer-facing idea: performance, deck movement, onboard comfort, and control. This makes the page easier to scan and more persuasive.

Performance

Born for high-speed cruising.

Carbon-rich construction, a deep-V hull, and twin MTU 16V 2000 M96L engines give the 8X the speed-first character Pershing buyers expect, with a maximum speed listed at 48 knots.

Deck lifestyle

Movement from cockpit to water feels natural.

The stern platform, cockpit, sport-fly access, and wide side decks create a page story around how the yacht is actually used: boarding, lounging, entertaining, swimming, and moving safely through the boat.

Design signature

Aerodynamic lines with functional space.

The model page calls out Pershing's side wings, integrated sport-fly stair, glass-heavy salon, and low aggressive profile as design choices that also improve usability and deck flow.

Layout

Show how the yacht works before listing every detail.

A buyer needs to understand the living plan: where people gather, where guests sleep, how crew is separated, and how the deck connects to the water. This section turns raw specs into spatial understanding.

Sport-fly and sun deck

Upper relaxation zone with sunpad, seating, teak, handrails, lighting, and independent audio.

Main deck

Integrated cockpit, salon, helm, bow lounge, side walkways, aft garage, and swim platform.

Lower deck

Four guest cabins, three guest bathrooms, crew accommodation, and refined interior living below deck.

Tender and water access

Garage, hydraulic platform, hideaway swim ladder, gangway, and stern systems are grouped as a lifestyle feature.

Standard equipment

Detailed lists belong in expandable groups.

Serious buyers can still access the equipment depth, but casual visitors are not forced through hundreds of bullets. Each group maps to a real onboard area.

Upper Sun Deck
  • Carbon fiber and teak-laid stairs with stainless steel handrail.
  • GRP and stainless steel gate, LED lights, and plexiglass windshield.
  • Sunpad, sofa with lockers and glass holders, teak, and waterproof speakers.
Cockpit and Aft Deck
  • Barbecue, sink, teak table, GRP seating, cockpit lighting, and audio.
  • Access to engine room, stern sunbathing area, mooring stations, and aft garage.
  • Hydraulic swim platform, gangway, external shower, and hideaway swim ladder.
Helm and Navigation
  • Multifunction touch displays, autopilot, monitoring system, radar, GPS, and VHF.
  • Bow thruster joystick, electronic levers, trim systems, and surface propeller controls.
  • Frau leather helm seating, carbon fiber panels, sliding roof, and windshield systems.
Salon
  • Audio-video system, hideaway smart TV, Apple TV, and surround sound.
  • Dining table, Frau leather chairs, sofa, parquet floor, electric curtains, and A/C.
  • Side windows, glassware and cutlery storage, icemaker, fridge, and refined wall finishes.
Guest Cabins
  • Master cabin, VIP cabin, and two double guest cabins with dedicated AV systems.
  • Premium beds, wardrobes, portholes, mirrors, reading lights, A/C, and soft-touch switches.
  • Guest bathrooms with parquet floors, electric WCs, mirrors, showers, and storage.
Systems and Safety
  • Fire systems, emergency engine shutdown controls, shore power, shore water, and fuel systems.
  • Windlass, anchor, chain wash-down, mooring cleats, fairleads, radar arch, and navigation lights.
  • Specifications and standard equipment are subject to change by model and configuration.
